Introduction to Differential Topology

A book in preparation

Differential topology
Book
Smooth manifolds, transversality, vector bundles, degree theory, the Poincaré–Hopf theorem and differential forms, up to Stokes’ theorem: a book for graduate students, with interactive figures and training for qualifying exams.
Author
Affiliation

Lucas Ozaki Mizuguti

IBILCE-UNESP

Published

November 15, 2026

I am writing this book for graduate students taking a first course in differential topology, the subject that many programs call Differentiable Manifolds or Smooth Manifolds. Its goal is to help them understand the ideas behind the definitions and theorems, and to prepare them for their qualifying exams.

Its references are the classical books of the area, such as Milnor (1965), Spivak (1965), Hirsch (1976), Guillemin and Pollack (2010), Lima (2007) and Lee (2012).

Status: in preparation. The book is not available yet; it will be published on this site when it is ready.

How the book is organized

Each chapter has four kinds of sections.

  • Theory, with interactive figures. In most figures you can drag the points, move the sliders and start animations, and the figures in space can be rotated.
  • Exercises at the end of each section.
  • Extra topics, such as the equivalence of the definitions of tangent space, Whitney’s embedding theorem, the hairy ball theorem and manifolds with corners.
  • Training for the oral part of qualifying exams, at the end of each chapter: questions of the kind asked in the oral part of a qualifying exam, to be answered out loud, without looking at the text.

Prerequisites

Advanced calculus in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) (the inverse and implicit function theorems, the local form of immersions and submersions, the rank theorem) and basic general topology (Hausdorff spaces, countable bases, compactness, connectedness and the quotient topology).

Contents

Part I · Manifolds and smooth maps

  1. Smooth manifolds
    Topological manifolds, atlases and changes of coordinates, smooth structures, existence, uniqueness and classification, non-Hausdorff manifolds.
  2. Examples, quotient manifolds and smooth maps
    Spheres and stereographic projection, products and graphs, quotient manifolds (the torus, real projective space), Lie groups and properly discontinuous actions, smooth maps and diffeomorphisms.
  3. Tangent spaces
    Tangent vectors as classes of curves and as derivations, the differential and the chain rule, the equivalence of the definitions.

Part II · Immersions, submersions and transversality

  1. Immersions, submersions and submanifolds
    The inverse function theorem and the local forms, embeddings, submanifolds, the regular value theorem, Sard’s theorem, immersed submanifolds and the Kronecker curve on the torus.
  2. Transversality
    Maps transversal to a submanifold, inverse images of submanifolds, stable properties and the transversality theorem.

Part III · Bundles, vector fields and orientation

  1. Vector bundles
    Vector bundles and transition functions, the tangent bundle, sections and frames, subbundles.
  2. Vector fields
    The Lie bracket, integral curves and flows, singularities, parallelizable manifolds, left-invariant fields on Lie groups.
  3. Orientation
    Orientation of vector spaces, of manifolds and of vector bundles, normal vector fields, tubular neighborhoods.
  4. Partitions of unity and applications
    Bump functions and partitions of unity, approximation of continuous maps, Whitney’s embedding theorems, Riemannian metrics, the classification of manifolds of dimension 1.

Part IV · Degree, intersection and Euler characteristic

  1. Manifolds with boundary
    The tangent space at boundary points, the induced orientation on the boundary, regular values, manifolds of dimension 1 with boundary, Brouwer’s fixed point theorem.
  2. Intersection theory and degree
    Transversality and homotopies, intersection number and degree modulo 2, the Brouwer degree, the fundamental theorem of algebra, the hairy ball theorem.
  3. Euler characteristic and the Poincaré–Hopf theorem
    The index of a singularity, triangulations and the classification of compact surfaces, the Poincaré–Hopf theorem.

Part V · Differential forms and integration

  1. The cotangent bundle and 1-forms
    The cotangent bundle, 1-forms and work, the pullback, line integrals, the gradient on a Riemannian manifold.
  2. Multilinear algebra and \(k\)-forms
    Tensors, alternating tensors and the wedge product, tensor fields and differential \(k\)-forms.
  3. Orientation and the exterior derivative
    Forms in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) and \(\mathbb{R}^3\), volume forms, the exterior derivative, closed and exact forms, de Rham cohomology and the Poincaré lemma.
  4. Integration and Stokes’ theorem
    Integration on manifolds, Stokes’ theorem, manifolds with corners, densities.
  5. Lie derivative, interior product and Cartan’s formula
    The Lie derivative, the interior product, Cartan’s magic formula, divergence and curl.
